# Informational keyword research: aitimeline.world

All search-volume figures below are reasoned relative estimates. No volume API is
connected to this project, so demand is expressed only as High / Medium / Low tiers
based on observed SERP competition, autocomplete patterns, "People also ask" coverage,
and the breadth of pages currently ranking. Treat tiers as directional, not absolute.

Scope: informational / educational ("blog") search intent only. Queries people type to
learn about AI history, milestones, people, labs, models, eras, and concepts. Every
keyword here can be honestly served by existing site content (81 milestones, 86 people,
57 organizations, 11 eras, 7 categories, 3 editorial pillar pages) unless flagged as a
content gap.

## How to read the table

- **Intent**: always informational for this site (kept in the table for completeness).
- **Demand**: reasoned estimate (High / Medium / Low).
- **Difficulty**: ranking difficulty (Low / Med / High). Head terms are dominated by
  Wikipedia, Britannica, IBM, Coursera, TechTarget, Built In, DataCamp (these are High).
  Specific milestone/person/org/era queries are far more winnable.
- **Winnable?**: can existing content honestly rank? Yes / Partial (needs a dedicated
  page or more depth) / Stretch (head term, brand-new site, long game).
- **Page type**: home, era, milestone, category, year, tag, person, organization, or
  pillar (`/history/[slug]`).
- **Target page**: existing route or a proposed slug (proposed pages are marked NEW).

Existing pillar pages today: `/history/history-of-openai`,
`/history/history-of-ai-agents`, `/history/most-important-ai-milestones`.

## Notes on strategy

- The cheapest wins are on-page: milestone, person, era, and organization pages already
  exist for most Top 25 entries. Tighten their H1, title tag, and opening paragraph to
  match the exact query (for example `/timeline/chatgpt-2022` should answer "when was
  ChatGPT released" in the first sentence and lead the meta description with the date).
- Head terms ("history of artificial intelligence", "AI timeline") are dominated by
  Wikipedia, Britannica, Coursera, IBM, and TechTarget. Compete on these with the home
  page plus a dedicated `history-of-artificial-intelligence` pillar, but treat them as a
  long game, not a quick win.
- The 12 proposed pillars cluster naturally into a hub-and-spoke: `evolution-of-ai` is the
  hub linking to era pages and the concept pillars (deep learning, neural networks, ML,
  LLMs, generative AI). Internal links from pillars down to individual milestone pages
  should carry the topical authority.
- Every proposed page is grounded only in existing milestones. No keyword above asks the
  site to cover an entity or event that is not already in the dataset.
